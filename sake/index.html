Why sake

Sake is made from just rice, water, koji and yeast, yet small decisions about polishing, fermentation temperature and yeast change the result enormously. That mix of science and craft is what hooked me. In my opinion, it’s also the best drink to pair with any food.

I took the WSET Level 3 Award in Sake in 2025. Since then I’ve taught classes and led tastings, and I’d like more people in the UK to try it.

Glossary

麹kōji
KojiSteamed rice grown with the mould Aspergillus oryzae. Its enzymes turn the rice’s starch into sugars that yeast can ferment.
吟醸ginjō
GinjoA premium grade made from rice polished to 60% or less of its original size and fermented slowly at low temperatures, often giving fruity, floral aromas.
精米歩合seimai-buai
Polishing ratioThe percentage of each rice grain that remains after milling. At 50%, half of the grain has been polished away.

Qualifications and awards

The Wine & Spirit Education Trust (WSET) is the largest global provider of drinks qualifications. Its Level 3 Award in Sake is an advanced qualification covering production, styles and systematic tasting.

Class & tasting

An introduction to sake

A two-hour class and tasting for twelve guests, several of them wine experts. I prepared slides and tasting sheets, took the group through the basics of sake and how to taste it, then answered questions that went deep into the technical side: how sake’s chemical composition differs from wine’s, fining, how the shubo (yeast starter) is made, and the role of different yeast strains.

My friend Jennifer Yeung sourced the sake and served the guests. Thank you also to Laura Kent, founder of Yorkshire Wine School, for lending their classroom to teach the class in.

Connecting Japanese producers with the UK

The UK needs more sake, and more drinkers who learn to enjoy its full variety of styles.

I don’t currently run an import or distribution business, but I’m very interested in talking with Japanese breweries, UK importers and distributors, bars and restaurants about bringing more sake to the UK.
